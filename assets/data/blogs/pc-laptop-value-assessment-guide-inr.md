# What's Your PC Really Worth? A Value Assessment Guide for Indian Buyers

Most Indian SMBs buy hardware based on brand names and sales pitches. They overpay for specs they don't need and underinvest in the components that actually matter. The result? Systems that slow down within a year, teams that wait for renders, and infrastructure bills that bleed margins.

At VVG ONLINE, we've seen this pattern across hundreds of businesses. **The fix isn't spending more — it's spending smarter**. Here's how to calculate the real value of a PC or laptop, in rupees.

## Why This Matters for Your Business

Your team's productivity is directly tied to the hardware they use. A ₹80,000 laptop that can't handle your workflow is more expensive than a ₹1,20,000 machine that finishes tasks in half the time. The math is simple, but most businesses never do it.

Worse, many SMBs are now discovering that running private AI models locally — for customer support, data analysis, content generation — requires specific hardware capabilities. Buying the wrong machine today means buying again tomorrow.

## The Components That Actually Matter

### CPU (Central Processing Unit)

The brain of your system. But bigger numbers don't always mean better value.

- **Clock Speed (GHz):** Higher generally means faster, but architecture matters more. A newer AMD Ryzen 5 at 3.7GHz can outperform an older Intel i7 at 4.2GHz (and is more power efficient).
- **Cores/Threads:** More cores = better multitasking. For business workloads (multiple Chrome tabs, CRM, email, spreadsheet), 6-8 cores is the sweet spot. For video editing or 3D rendering, 12-16 cores (should do the job).
- **Architecture:** AMD Ryzen 7000/9000 series and Intel's latest (Panther lake) gen offer significantly better performance-per-watt. This translates to lower electricity bills for your office.

**INR Reality:** Import duties add 15-30% to CPU costs. An AMD Ryzen 7 7700X costs roughly ₹30,000-35,000 in India versus $299 (₹28,600) in the US. The gap is smaller than you think — but only if you buy at the right time. Wait for deals/offers.

### GPU (Graphics Processing Unit)

Critical for gaming, video editing, 3D rendering — and increasingly, for running AI models locally/privately.

- **VRAM (Video RAM):** This is what determines whether you can run AI models. 8GB VRAM can handle 7B parameter models comfortably. 16GB VRAM handles 13B models. 24GB VRAM handles 30B+ models. For private AI, VRAM is the most important spec.
- **CUDA Cores / Stream Processors:** More cores = faster parallel processing. NVIDIA's RTX 4060 (3,072 CUDA shader cores, 96 tensor cores for AI and machine learning acceleration, and 24 ray tracing (RT) cores for real-time ray tracing effects in games) offers excellent value at (current market price) ₹45,000-50,000.
- **Architecture:** RTX 40-series and AMD RX 7000-series are current gen. Older RTX 30-series still offers great value if bought used.

**The Private AI Angle:** If you're planning to run local AI models (which every SMBs should be considering in 2026), your GPU choice determines what you can run. An RTX 3060 12GB at ₹42,000-45,000 (current market price) is the minimum viable option for private AI. An AMD RX 9060 XT 16GB at ₹60,000-65,000 (current market price) is the sweet spot.

### RAM (Random Access Memory)

- **16GB:** Minimum for modern business use. Your team will feel the slowdown with 8GB.
- **32GB:** Recommended for any team running multiple applications simultaneously, or for AI workloads.
- **64GB+:** Only if you're doing heavy video editing, 3D rendering, or running large AI models locally.

**INR Tip:** DDR5 RAM prices have dropped significantly. 32GB DDR5 5600MHz DIMM costs ₹10,000-15,000 in India. Don't cheap out here — it's the cheapest upgrade with the biggest impact. If your budget allows go for ECC RAM.

### Storage (SSD vs HDD)

- **SSD is non-negotiable.** Your primary drive must be an NVMe SSD. Boot times, application loading, file transfers — everything improves.
- **1TB NVMe SSD GEN4:** ~₹20,000 in India. This should be your baseline.
- **HDD:** Only for bulk storage (backups, archives). Never for your OS or active projects.

### Motherboard

The foundation. Don't overspend, but don't cheap out either.

- **For Intel:** B760 or Z790 chipset covers most business needs. Prefer this for creative workflows.
- **For AMD:** B650 or X670E offers excellent upgrade paths. Prefer this for gaming.
- **Key:** Ensure enough PCIe, M.2 slots for future expansion, and enough RAM slots (4, not 2).

### Power Supply (PSU)

- **650W 80+ Bronze:** Sufficient for most business builds with mid-range GPUs.
- **750W 80+ Gold:** If you're running a high-end GPU or plan to upgrade later.
- **Never cheap out on PSU.** A bad PSU can kill your entire system. ₹6,000-10,000 for a reliable unit is insurance, not expense. If poosible buy Modular PSU.

## Benchmarking: How to Compare

Don't trust marketing claims. Trust numbers.

### CPU Benchmarks

- **PassMark:** Standardized scores across all CPUs. Higher = better. [passmark.com/benchmarks/cpu](https://www.passmark.com/benchmarks/cpu)
- **Cinebench:** Real-world rendering performance. Great for comparing multi-core performance. [cinebench.com](https://www.cinebench.com/)

### GPU Benchmarks

- **3DMark:** Industry standard for GPU testing. [3dmark.com](https://www.3dmark.com/)
- **Real-world FPS:** The best benchmark is your actual workload. Test with your actual applications.

### Laptop-Specific

- **Notebookcheck:** The gold standard for laptop reviews. Includes thermal throttling data, battery life under load, and real-world performance. [notebookcheck.net](https://www.notebookcheck.net/)

## The Value Formula

Here's how to calculate value per rupee:

**Performance Score ÷ Price = Performance per Rupee**

### Example Calculation

| Laptop | PassMark Score | Price (INR) | Value Score |
| ------ | -------------- | ----------- | ----------- |
| Option A | 15,000 | ₹76,600 | 0.196 |
| Option B | 16,000 | ₹86,100 | 0.186 |
| Option C | 18,500 | ₹95,700 | 0.193 |

Option A has the best value-per-rupee, but Option C offers the best absolute performance. Your choice depends on whether you prioritize cost or capability.

### For Business Use

- **General productivity:** Focus on CPU and RAM. GPU doesn't matter much.
- **Content creation:** GPU and CPU cores are equally important.
- **Private AI:** GPU VRAM is the #1 factor. CPU+SSD matters for model loading speed.
- **Development:** RAM and CPU cores. Storage speed matters for large codebases.

## Beyond Specs: What Actually Matters

Raw benchmarks don't tell the whole story. For business use, consider:

### Cooling

Better cooling = sustained performance. A laptop that throttles after 10 minutes of load is worthless for demanding tasks. Check thermal reviews before buying.

### Build Quality

A well-built laptop lasts 4-5 years. A cheap one might last 2. The ₹20,000 difference is recovered in longevity alone.

### Screen Quality

Your team stares at screens 8+ hours a day. A bad screen causes fatigue, headaches, and reduced productivity. Look for:
- **IPS panel** minimum (not TN)
- **300+ nits brightness** for comfortable use
- **100% sRGB color accuracy** for design work
- **120Hz+ refresh rate** for smoother scrolling

### Ports and Connectivity

- **USB-C with DisplayPort:** Future-proofing
- **Ethernet port:** Reliable office connectivity
- **HDMI 2.1:** For external displays
- **SD card reader:** If your team works with cameras

### Resale Value

Apple MacBooks Pro hold value best. ThinkPad and Dell XPS retain value well in the Indian market. Budget Android laptops depreciate fastest.
